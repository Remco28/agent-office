Report on the review-loop skill (read-only; I changed nothing). I read SKILL.md, briefs.md, evidence.md, profiles/_template.md, profiles/explainer-public.md, and the stop-rule parts of profiles/code-review.md.

1. What it is and how it works

It's a procedure for getting a stranger's reading of something I made, then being strict about which of their answers I believe. It targets two things: coherence (contradicts itself or its source) and comprehension (does a stranger get it). It is blind to merit, so a human reviews at the end and I must say which of the three I'm reporting on. It's heavy, costs many agents and rounds, and runs only when the user asks. If the work looks important, ask first with a rough size.

The loop: (0) run mechanical checks myself, re-runnable scripts against a source of truth. (1) Write one success sentence about the reader's task, and get the house rules/facts confirmed by the person who knows. (2) Pick a profile (audience cast, house rules, stop rule), or copy the template. (3) Freeze the artifact, give readers the rendered version, run them in parallel and blind, across models where possible, with a six-part brief that asks "did it work for you?" rather than "find problems". (4) Sort findings into tiers: tier 1 is verified by two independent readers or checked against the artifact (fix); tier 2 is one reader plus an outside source, sent to a second agent to try to kill it; tier 3 is taste (collect for the human, don't act); tier 0 is an artifact of how I showed it (reject). (5) Fix verified items, read tier 3 for "too long" signals, prove fixes by counting old wording to zero and using the thing as its audience would, route choices that change outcomes to the human, and write predictions in the ledger before each round. Throughout: a disposition auditor checks my ratings every round before any stop decision, and the ledger lives in review-loop/LEDGER.md, with a "Believed, and wrong" line each round. Stop when two rounds have no tier-1 finding, or when the rest is taste, or when the budget is spent. Then hand to the human.

2. Plan for the waiver form (one page, youth club, lawyer away two weeks, pasted text, no project folder)

Honest first point: the skill's own rules say the rules must come from the person who knows, and that person is unreachable. So I'd tell the user before starting that the review can check coherence and comprehension, but cannot check that the waiver is legally sufficient, and the print run shouldn't be treated as lawyer-approved. Step 1 says that if the knowing person can't be reached, mark the rules unconfirmed in the ledger and tell the user. Ask whether they want to wait for the lawyer, or print with a marked "unreviewed by counsel" caveat, which is their call.

Concretely:
- Save the pasted text to a file in my scratchpad (no project folder, so the ledger goes to scratch space, and I ask the user where they'd like it kept).
- Ask for the one-sentence success question. My draft: "A volunteer who has never seen this form can say, after one read, what they are agreeing to, what they must not do, and who to contact." Confirm with the user.
- Get the facts from the user: dates, the club name, the activities, the contact, the age limits, the insurer or the medical wording. Mark them as confirmed by the user, not by the lawyer.
- Step 0: check what can be checked: names, dates and phone numbers are consistent across the form; no blanks or placeholders left; every field the signature block refers to exists; the form is printable on one page (render it to PDF and count pages).
- Choose a profile. None fits. Copy profiles/explainer-public.md or the template, and write house rules with tests. Examples: "every signature line is preceded by the statement it applies to"; "a parent/guardian line exists for anyone under the age stated in the form"; "the form names one emergency contact and one way to withdraw consent". Keep it to three to six rules.
- Size: a one-page form needs about three comprehension readers (a volunteer with no legal background, a parent of a child in the club, and a non-native English reader at roughly B2), one specialist for internal consistency, and a disposition auditor, one round, maybe a second round after fixes. Ask the user to confirm the budget first.
- Briefs: the six-part shape, the success question, and "did this work for you?" plus "what does it ask you to agree to, in your own words?" That second question is the comprehension test. Name the worry: "Is anything here a promise the club can't keep?" Do not ask them to find legal problems; they can't, and asking invites invented ones.
- Freeze the rendered PDF, record its hash in the ledger.
- Run readers blind across models where I can. Sort the findings. Fix only verified items. Route any change to what the form obligates the club or the volunteer to (a liability limit, a photo-consent default, a medical clause) to the user as a choice, not an edit. Don't invent legal wording.
- Predictions in the ledger before the next round. Disposition audit before stopping.
- Hand back to the user with the caveat in the first line: comprehension and internal consistency were reviewed; legal sufficiency was not, and the lawyer should see it before anything is signed.

3. Problems, gaps, and contradictions in the skill

- Tension with the "print" stakes: the skill says "if it looks important, ask the user first", but it doesn't say what to do when the stakes are high AND the rule-owner is unavailable. Step 1 says "mark unconfirmed and tell the user", which is fine, but nothing says whether to proceed or stop when the form is a legal document. I'd have to decide that myself. I'd ask the user first.
- No profile fits a legal or form document. The template says to copy the closest profile, but the closest (explainer-public) has rules about non-detection and association that don't transfer. The "two profiles" rule doesn't help here.
- Step 0's "source of truth" can't cover a waiver. Most of the checks are about consistency, which the skill covers, but the skill assumes the rules exist somewhere to check against. The lawyer is that source, and it's absent.
- "Ask whether it worked, not what's wrong" conflicts a little with "tier 1 needs two independent readers". For a one-page form, a reader who "gets it" may still be wrong about what they are agreeing to. The skill's own comprehension test ("say it in your own words") has no check for a reader who is confidently wrong. The recall test is named but not written as a brief.
- Stop rules are sized for long artifacts and code. "Two rounds with no tier-1 finding" might be reached in one round for a one-pager with no readers catching anything, which could be false comfort. The skill has no minimum-reader count for a short piece.
- Ledger location: "in the project" with no project folder is handled only by "ask the user where, or keep it in your scratch space". Scratch space is session-scoped, so the ledger could be lost. That's not a good fit for a multi-round job.
- The disposition auditor is "never skipped", but it audits the author's ratings against the triage rules. For a non-code artifact, there are no written severity rules, so the auditor has nothing to check against. The skill says the auditor checks "the triage rules the author claims to follow", so I'd have to write them first. The skill doesn't say that.
- Model spread: "spread across models where you can", but it doesn't say what to do when only one model is available. I'd record that.
- The step-4 table says "Two or more independent readers, or checked against the artifact (including reproducing it yourself)" for tier 1, but for a form the "check against the artifact" is mostly my own reading, so it's a loose tier-1 gate. The skill's warning that "two of twelve same-model readers may be one habit counted twice" applies here too.
- Minor: the SKILL.md description says "Heavy, so use it only when the user asks for it", while the body says "if something looks important enough, ask the user first". A user asking for a review isn't asking for a legal sign-off, and the skill doesn't separate those.
- The Step 3 brief skeleton's fifth part (the honesty line) and the sixth part (known artifacts) are fine, but the "known quirks" for a plain text form are unclear: the rendering could alter layout, and nothing says whether readers should see the PDF or the text.
- The cost reader and A/B reader are optional and don't apply to a single form unless there's a second draft, so I'd skip them. The skill doesn't say explicitly that the fix auditor is needed from round 2 even for a small job.
- evidence.md is extensive but describes one author and two artifacts. Its claims are presented as hypotheses, which is good. Its episodes are all long-form or code, so the stop rules for a one-page form are untested.
